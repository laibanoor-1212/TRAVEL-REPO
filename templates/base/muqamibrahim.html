Maqam-e-Ibrahim

Maqam-e-Ibrahim refers to the stone that served as a movable scaffold for Hazrat Ibrahim (AS) during the construction of the Kaaba. As the walls rose higher, the stone miraculously rose as well, allowing him to continue building.

The stone is currently housed in a beautiful gold and crystal hexagonal pavilion. The imprints of Hazrat Ibrahim's (AS) feet are clearly visible on the stone, measuring about 27 cm in length. This is considered one of the many clear signs (Ayat-ul-Bayyinat) mentioned in the Quran regarding the sanctity of Makkah.

Following the completion of Tawaf, it is a Sunnah and a command of Allah to perform two Rakat of prayer behind Maqam-e-Ibrahim, if space permits. The Quran states: "And take, [O believers], from the standing place of Abraham a place of prayer" (Surah Al-Baqarah, 2:125).

Historical & Spiritual Facts

  • Miraculous Texture: The stone, which was once hard, became soft like wax to take the impression of the Prophet's ﷺ feet.
  • Placement: Originally, the stone was attached to the Kaaba wall but was moved further away by Hazrat Umar (RA) to facilitate Tawaf.
  • Prayer Ritual: If the area is crowded, pilgrims can offer the two Rakat anywhere in the Masjid al-Haram while keeping the Maqam between them and the Kaaba.
  • Design: The current golden casing was installed to protect the stone while allowing pilgrims to view the imprints through the glass.

SPIRITUAL SIGNIFICANCE

Divine Legacy

It stands as a physical testimony to the dedication of Hazrat Ibrahim (AS) and his son Hazrat Ismail (AS) in serving Allah.

SACRED MARKER

Place of Prayer

Offering Salah here signifies the connection between the follower and the legacy of the Father of Prophets.

A Clear Sign

Mentioned specifically in the Quran as a sign of the holiness of the Sanctuary in Makkah.
